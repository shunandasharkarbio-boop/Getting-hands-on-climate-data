# 🌡️ Temperature Anomalies

## 📖 Overview

In climate science, **temperature anomalies** are often more informative than absolute temperatures. A temperature anomaly represents the difference between an observed temperature and a long-term average (known as the **baseline**).

The baseline is typically calculated using **30 years or more** of historical temperature data, providing a reliable reference for detecting climate change over time.

- 🔺 **Positive anomaly** → The observed temperature is **warmer** than the baseline.
- 🔻 **Negative anomaly** → The observed temperature is **cooler** than the baseline.

Because anomalies remove the influence of local climate differences, they are widely used to compare temperature changes across regions and over long periods.

## 📊 What are Climate Stripes?

Climate stripes represent annual temperature anomalies using a sequence of colored bars:

- 🔵 **Blue** — Cooler than the long-term average
- ⚪ **White / Neutral** — Close to the baseline average
- 🔴 **Red** — Warmer than the long-term average

This visualization clearly highlights long-term warming or cooling trends without requiring numerical interpretation.
